Het uitgangspunt

Two things shape this plan, and neither is a sight. The first is the 06:00 landing: you have roughly eight hours in the city before a hotel room exists, with three children who have not slept properly. Day one is therefore built backwards from a compulsory afternoon rest, and the night market is deliberately postponed. The second is the light. In late December the sun sets before 17:50, so the street life you came for happens in a narrow window that runs straight into a three-year-old's bedtime. Every evening here eats first and browses after.

Wat dit plan stuurt

  • Dark by 17:50, so evenings are short: eat before you browse, never after
  • No hotel room before 14:00 on arrival day; the whole of day one is built around that
  • 15 to 20C but the windiest month of the year, and nothing indoors is heated
  • 25 and 26 December are the only holidays; 28 to 31 December are normal working days

Cha chaan teng breakfast

Australia Dairy Company

47 Parkes Street, Jordan

Hong Kong breakfast at its most efficient and least gentle, and a fifteen minute walk from the hotel once the bags are dropped. You will be seated with strangers and your plates will be cleared while you are still chewing. That pace is the only real problem with three small children, so be there by 07:45 before the Sunday queue builds and accept that you are being moved along.

  • Closed on Thursdays, which rules it out on your departure morning
  • The English menu exists but you have to ask for it
  • Small and loud; leave the stroller outside with one adult

Aan tafel

  • Scrambled eggs on toast, the reason the place exists
  • Steamed milk pudding, split one between the children
  • Macaroni and ham soup, safe for cautious eaters
09:002 hours

Park

Kowloon Park

22 Austin Road, Tsim Sha Tsui

The point is not the aviary or the flamingos, it is a large fenced space where jetlagged children can run themselves flat before the room is ready. Open from 05:00, free, and five minutes downhill from breakfast. Toilets are clean but spaced far apart, so use them when you see them rather than when you need them.

  • Playground and the bird lake are both nearer the Austin Road end
  • The swimming pools are a separate paid facility and not worth the detour today
  • Aim to be back at the hotel by 11:00 whether or not the room is ready
15:301 hour 15

Shopping street

Shanghai Street kitchenware shops

Shanghai Street between Kansu Street and Argyle Street, Yau Ma Tei

A working supply street rather than a market: cleavers, woks, bamboo steamers and chopping blocks sold mostly to restaurants. It is the gentlest possible reintroduction to the city after a nap, because it is flat, short and requires nothing of the children. Several shops are cash only and most shut around 19:00, and anything sharp has to go in checked luggage on the way home.

  • Start at the Kansu Street end and walk north, the good shops thin out after Argyle Street
  • Prices are marked; haggling is not the custom here
17:001 hour

Wonton noodles

Mak Man Kee Noodle Shop

51 Parkes Street, Jordan

Shrimp wonton noodles three minutes from the hotel, Michelin-listed and priced like a canteen. There is no minimum spend per person, which is exactly what makes it workable with five of you. The bowls are genuinely small, so order more than seems sensible, and eat at 17:00 rather than later because the room is tiny and fills fast.

  • Open from 12:00 to 00:30, so it also works as a late fallback on any other night
  • Ask for the noodles slightly undercooked if the children dislike a firm bite

Aan tafel

  • Shrimp wonton noodle soup
  • Braised pork knuckle, enough for two children

Dim sum

One Dim Sum

209A Tung Choi Street, Prince Edward

It opens at 09:00, which is unusually early for dim sum and the whole reason it sits here rather than at lunch. Queues build hard from 11:00 and the wait is outdoors. Order in two rounds rather than one, because the kitchen is faster than five people can eat and a table of cold dim sum is a waste of the meal.

  • Two minutes from Prince Edward MTR exit B2, which is where the rest of the morning starts
  • Tick the paper order sheet; nobody will talk you through the menu

Aan tafel

  • Har gow and siu mai
  • Barbecue pork buns, the safest thing on the sheet for children
  • Rice noodle rolls
10:1545 min

Market

Yuen Po Street Bird Garden

Yuen Po Street, Prince Edward

Older men bring their songbirds here in carved bamboo cages, mostly in the morning; by mid-afternoon it empties out. Children want to poke at the cages and the owners will not tolerate it, so say that before you walk in rather than after. Live crickets are sold as feed, which some children find riveting and others find upsetting.

  • Walk in from the Boundary Street end, it is quieter
  • Twenty minutes is often enough; it is small
11:1545 min

Market street

Flower Market Road

Flower Market Road, Prince Edward

Two hundred metres of florists that sell wholesale and retail at the same counter, and the only street in Kowloon that smells good. Buckets and crates take up most of the pavement, so a stroller is a genuine nuisance here and a carrier is better. Late December stock is orchids and poinsettias, which is modest compared to the fortnight before Lunar New Year.

  • Connects directly to the bird garden at its eastern end
  • Cut flowers cannot come home with you; buy nothing you have to carry for four days
12:1545 min

Market street

Goldfish Street

Tung Choi Street north, Mong Kok

Aquarium shops with bagged fish hung in rows across the shopfronts, a straight walk south from the flower market. Most shops open around 10:30, which is why this comes after the other two rather than before. Shopkeepers are short with children who touch the bags, and several also sell turtles and amphibians that invite exactly that.

  • Ends at Mong Kok MTR, which is where you head back for the rest
  • Do not step inside the narrow shops with all three children at once
15:301 hour 45

Shopping street

Sham Shui Po Toy StreetMust

Fuk Wing Street, Sham Shui Po

A full block of toy and stationery shops that sell to Hong Kong parents rather than to visitors, which is why the prices are what they are. Shops are narrow and stacked to the ceiling, so go in one adult with one child at a time instead of all five. Most are cash only, none will gift-wrap, and quality ranges from decent to disposable with no way to tell from the packaging.

  • Open roughly 10:30 to 20:00
  • Late December stock leans festive; the wider toy range is better outside the holidays
  • Set a per-child budget before you arrive, not after they have seen the shelves
17:151 hour

Bamboo-pressed noodles

Lau Sum Kee Noodle

80 Fuk Wing Street, Sham Shui Po

Noodles still pressed under a bamboo pole, served in a room with about eight tables. Portions are small by European standards and the staff will move you on once the bowls are empty, so this is a forty-five minute meal, not an evening. If it is full, the same family runs a second branch a corner away on Kweilin Street.

  • Cash is safest
  • Open from 12:00; before 18:00 you have a reasonable chance of a table

Aan tafel

  • Dry tossed noodles with shrimp roe
  • Wonton noodle soup
  • Braised mushrooms, mild enough for the youngest
18:2030 min

Dessert

Kung Wo Beancurd Factory

118 Pei Ho Street, Sham Shui Po

Tofu pudding and fried tofu from an operation that has been grinding soybeans here since the nineteenth century, eaten on stools with the alley in view. Treat it as fifteen or twenty minutes of dessert, not a second dinner. Bring your own napkins and do not expect the seating to be comfortable or particularly clean at a busy hour.

  • Open 07:00 to 21:00
  • Order the tofu pudding warm in December, not chilled
  • Sham Shui Po MTR is three minutes away for the ride home

Aan tafel

  • Tofu pudding with brown sugar syrup
  • Fried tofu puffs
  • Fresh soy milk

Theme park and aquarium

Ocean ParkMust

Ocean Park Road, Aberdeen

A local institution rather than an import, which is the reason it is here and Disneyland is not: most of the queue around you will be Hong Kong families. Late December sits inside the school holidays, so a Tuesday is still busy and being at the gate before opening is worth an hour later on. The park is split across a hill, the cable car between the halves has the longest wait of anything on site, and the Ocean Express funicular does the same job in four minutes.

  • Standard hours are 10:00 to 18:30 on a Tuesday, but holiday hours are often extended
  • Whiskers Harbour is the area built for the three-year-old; do it first while she still has legs
  • MTR South Island Line from Admiralty, two stops, rather than a taxi through the tunnel
  • The lower half is largely indoors, which is where to be if the wind picks up
18:451 hour 15

Cantonese roast meats

Yung's Tangerine Peel Roast Goose Restaurant

50-52 Portland Street, Yau Ma Tei

Roast goose, char siu and roast pork, served fast in a room where you may well share a table with strangers. It stays open until midnight, which is the actual reason it is on this evening rather than another: nobody knows what time you will get off that hill. The goose is fatty by design, so if that is a problem order char siu and soy chicken for the children instead.

  • Ten minutes on foot from the hotel, uphill along Nathan Road
  • Order the vegetables at the same time as the meat or they arrive last and cold

Aan tafel

  • Roast goose with tangerine peel
  • Char siu
  • Stir-fried choi sum with garlic

Ferry crossing

Star Ferry Pier, Tsim Sha TsuiMust

Tsim Sha Tsui Star Ferry Pier

Seven minutes across the harbour on a green and white boat that has been doing this since 1898, for a few Hong Kong dollars. Sit on the upper deck; the lower deck is enclosed and you see engine housing rather than skyline. Late December wind on open water is the coldest moment of the trip, so this is when the extra layer pays for itself.

  • Runs 06:30 to 23:30, every 6 to 12 minutes, tap Octopus at the turnstile
  • Seat backs flip, so you can turn them to face the way you are going
  • Lands at Central Pier 7, about fifteen minutes on foot from the first stop
09:3030 min

Wet market

Graham Street Market

Graham Street and Gage Street, Central

The oldest street market still running in Hong Kong, wedged on a slope in the middle of the banking district. Live fish, butchered pork and delivery trolleys all share one narrow lane, so hold hands and keep to one side rather than stopping in the middle. Half an hour is enough; it is a passage on the way to the temple, not a destination.

  • Roughly 08:00 to 18:00, best before 11:00
  • The Central to Mid-Levels escalator runs downhill until 10:00 and uphill after 10:30, so at this hour you walk up
  • Anyone squeamish about butchery should take the Gage Street side
10:1530 min

Temple

Man Mo Temple

124-130 Hollywood Road, Sheung Wan

Giant incense coils burn continuously overhead and the smoke is thick enough to sting your eyes within a few minutes. That is the reason to keep this to fifteen or twenty minutes with young children, and to skip it entirely if anyone is asthmatic. It is a working temple rather than a monument, so people at prayer are not photo subjects.

  • Open 08:00 to 18:00
  • Stand near the open doorway rather than under the coils
  • Hollywood Road outside is worth the walk afterwards, all antique shops and steep side stairs
12:301 hour 15

Beef brisket noodles

Kau Kee

21 Gough Street, Central

Braised beef brisket over noodles, in clear broth or in curry, from a room the size of a garage. It opens at 12:30 and not a minute earlier, the queue starts before that, it is cash only, and you will share a table. Order the clear broth for the children: the curry is genuinely strong and there is no mild version.

  • Closed Sundays, which is why this is a Wednesday
  • Five minutes downhill from Man Mo, so fill the gap on Hollywood Road rather than waiting outside
  • If the queue is absurd, Sing Heung Yuen on Mee Lun Street two streets away does tomato noodles

Aan tafel

  • Beef brisket noodles in clear broth
  • Beef tendon, if anyone is adventurous
13:4545 min

Tram ride

Ding ding tram from Western Market terminus

Western Market tram terminus, Des Voeux Road Central, Sheung Wan

A double-decker tram, slow and loud and about three dollars, with the front seat on the upper deck sitting at exactly child eye level above the traffic. Board at the terminus rather than mid-route so you actually get a choice of seats. You pay on the way out, not the way in, which catches almost everyone the first time.

  • Take an eastbound tram and get off at Admiralty for the park
  • Board from the back, exit at the front
  • Doors are narrow and the stairs are steep, so a folded stroller needs two hands
14:301 hour 15

Aviary

Edward Youde Aviary, Hong Kong Park

Hong Kong Park, 19 Cotton Tree Drive, Central

A free walk-through aviary on a raised wooden boardwalk, with birds flying loose around and above you. It closes at 17:00 and last entry is earlier than people assume, so this is an afternoon slot and not an evening one. The boardwalk is one-way and narrow, so a stroller is awkward and the three-year-old is better carried.

  • Open 09:00 to 17:00
  • The park itself has a decent playground if the aviary is over quickly
  • Uphill from Admiralty MTR, about ten minutes with children
16:451 hour

Waterfront walk

Tsim Sha Tsui Promenade

Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront, Salisbury Road

Come back across on the ferry and arrive at the Kowloon waterfront just as the light goes, which in late December is around 17:50. This is the same view people pay for from observation decks. The Symphony of Lights runs at 20:00, an hour past a realistic bedtime, so treat it as something you are deliberately not doing rather than something you are trying to fit in.

  • Coldest and windiest spot on the whole itinerary, dress for it
  • The stretch east of the clock tower is quieter than the Avenue of Stars
  • Fifteen minutes on foot up Nathan Road to Jordan, or one MTR stop
18:151 hour 45

Night market and street food

Temple Street Night Market

Temple Street, Jordan and Yau Ma Tei

This is the touristy one and you should go in knowing it; the reason it sits on the final evening rather than the first is that by now you know what things cost and buying something has become sensible. The Jordan end is food stalls, the middle is souvenirs, and stalls open from 14:00 but only look right after dark. Agree a price before anything is wrapped and check that what goes into the bag is what you pointed at.

  • Five minutes from the hotel, which is what makes an early exit possible
  • Eat at the Jordan end first, browse second, or the children will fade mid-negotiation
  • Kai Kai Dessert on Saigon Street is three minutes away and open until 01:00, for sesame soup or glutinous rice balls

Aan tafel

  • Grilled oysters at the food stalls
  • Clay pot rice, if anyone still has room

Departure

Kowloon Station, Airport Express

Austin Road West, West Kowloon

With an 11:00 flight you need to leave Jordan by 07:15 and there is no morning worth planning. For five people with luggage a taxi is both cheaper and simpler than five Airport Express fares plus the walk to the platform, so this stop is the alternative rather than the recommendation. Eat at the hotel: Australia Dairy is closed on Thursdays and nothing else useful opens that early.

  • Taxi from Jordan is roughly HKD 300 and 40 minutes at that hour
  • If you do take the Airport Express, it is 21 minutes from here and free shuttle buses run from hotels in Jordan
  • In-town check-in at this station has been suspended on and off; do not build the morning around it

Wat we hebben laten vallen

  • Victoria Peak and the Peak Tram, Queues routinely pass an hour in the school holidays, for a view a three-year-old will look at for ninety seconds
  • Hong Kong Disneyland, A second full theme-park day you do not have on four nights, and the opposite of the local street life you asked for
  • Big Buddha and the Ngong Ping cable car, Half a day of transit each way, and the cable car closes for high wind, which December supplies regularly
  • Tai O fishing village, The bus from Tung Chung is fifty minutes of hairpin bends; motion sickness with three children is a real risk, not a theoretical one
  • Ladies' Market, Tung Choi Street south, Same stock as Temple Street with harder selling; one night market is enough
  • Symphony of Lights, 20:00 nightly, Starts after the children are asleep, and the harbour at sunset is the better version of the same view
  • Apliu Street flea market, Second-hand electronics and bins of cable; nothing in it for a three-year-old, though it is two streets from the toy shops if you want twenty minutes
  • Sky100 and other observation decks, Paying for height in a city where the Star Ferry gives you the same skyline for the price of a coffee
  • Mido Cafe, The 1950s interior is the draw, but it closes on Wednesdays and the service is unpredictable enough that it is a poor bet with five people

Nog te bevestigen

  • Ocean Park's actual closing time on 29 December; the standard Tuesday close is 18:30 but school-holiday hours are frequently extended
  • Whether in-town check-in at Kowloon Station is running for your airline on 31 December; it has been suspended repeatedly and the counters close early in the day
  • Whether the harbourfront Christmas lighting is still up after 26 December; the 2026 WinterFest programme dates were not published at time of writing
  • Whether Child Octopus cards can now be issued digitally, or still require a counter and proof of age
  • Eaton HK's arrangement for five people in one room, and whether luggage can be held on the morning of 31 December
  • Sing Heung Yuen's hours, which are not reliably listed anywhere and vary with the owners
